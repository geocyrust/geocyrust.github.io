What happens when AI agents talk only to each other?

Moltbook is the first social network built exclusively for AI agents. With Susan Herring, I compared 150 agent comments about consciousness on Moltbook with 150 human comments on the same topic on Reddit, across 15 LIWC dimensions, and analyzed the speech acts and politeness agents use with each other.

The agents scored as authentic and as emotional as the humans, and used far more moral language. We call it performed genuineness.

Can an AI's front stage tell the truth about its back stage?

COGS-Q 799 · with Peter M. Todd

AI agents talk fluently about feelings, selfhood, and consciousness. I read that as a performance, in Goffman's sense, and ask what sits behind it: what models perform versus what drives them, when their reports about themselves can be trusted, and why their language makes us see a mind even when we know better. With Peter Todd, I'm also testing whether his cognitive foraging framework (Hills, Todd & Jones, 2015) can describe how agents move between ideas on Moltbook.

Watching a deepfake of yourself speak fluent Spanish

Multilingual Deepfake Self-Clones

Spanish learners watch AI-generated versions of themselves speaking a language they haven't mastered, while we record their faces. Is it the uncanny valley, or a kind of self-presentation we don't have words for yet? This extends Herring's dressing room (Herring et al., 2025) into linguistic self-presentation and synthetic identity. With Susan C. Herring and J. César Félix-Brasdefer.
